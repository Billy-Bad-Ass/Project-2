## PAGE 2 — THE CHART  
  
**⚠ Read this before the table**  
  
**Sound is the product of switch, plate and mounting architecture *interacting*. There is no single variable that "makes it thocky", and any guide promising one is overselling.**  
The same mod on two different boards gives different results. That is expected behaviour, not a fault in the mod or in you.

## PAGE 3 — What is reliable, and what depends on your board  
  
Most disappointment in this hobby comes from someone applying a mod that worked spectacularly in a video to a board where it had nothing to do.  
**Generally true, regardless of board**  
  
* **Foam reduces resonance.** Always. More foam, less ring.  
* **Denser and stiffer plates sound brighter.** Aluminium and brass up, polycarbonate and POM down.  
* **Flexible plates sound softer**, because they absorb through flex.  
* **Lubing removes friction noise.** It cannot fail to.  
* **Unlubed stabilisers rattle.** Universally.  
If a mod is in this list and it did nothing for you, you probably did it wrong or the board already had it.  
**Highly dependent on your specific board**  
  
* **How much a tape mod does.** Ranges from transformative to inaudible.  
* **Whether the board can sound "thocky" at all.** Some cannot, at any price.  
* **Whether adding foam helps or kills it.** A board that already ships well-dampened gets deadened rather than improved.  
* **Desk surface effects.** Depends on case weight, feet and how much sound exits downward.  
* **How much switch films matter.** Depends entirely on how loose the housings already were.  
**The most useful specific case**  
  
**A tape mod does far less on an already-dampened gasket-mount board than on a tray-mount board where the PCB screws straight to the case.**  
The tape works by adding a reflective layer under the PCB and damping case cavity resonance. A gasket board with foam has already dealt with most of that. There is simply less left to fix.  
**If you tape a gasket board and hear almost nothing, the mod worked exactly as it should have.** Do not add three more layers chasing a change that was never available. That is how people end up with four layers of masking tape inside a £300 keyboard for no acoustic reason at all.  
**The general principle behind all of it**  
  
Every mod on the chart does one of three things:  
1. **Removes an unwanted noise** — rattle, tick, scratch. *(Stabs, Holee, lube.)* These always work, because the noise was definitely there.  
2. **Removes resonance from an empty cavity.** *(Foam, tape.)* These work in proportion to how much empty cavity you had.  
3. **Changes the fundamental pitch of the impact.** *(Plate, mount, keycaps.)* These always work, and they are the ones you have to buy.  
**Category 2 is where all the disappointment lives.** If your board had no cavity resonance, nothing in category 2 will do anything, and no amount of tape will change that.  
  
## PAGE 4 — The order, and the expensive mistakes  
  
**Do it in this order**  
  
**1. Stabilisers first. Always first.**  
Rattle is the loudest and ugliest noise on most boards, and fixing it changes more than any acoustic mod on the chart. It is free, reversible and fast. Everything else is cosmetic by comparison.  
Doing this last is the most common sequencing mistake — people lube 90 switches, reassemble, and then discover the spacebar still rattles.  
**2. Foam second.**  
The biggest reliable change per unit of effort and risk. Start with case foam. Add plate foam if it is still too bright. Add PCB or PE foam only if you want to go further and you know PE is divisive.  
**3. Lube third.**  
The biggest single *improvement* in feel and sound, and by far the slowest step. **Budget 3–5 hours for a first full build including lubing.** It is also the one thing on this chart you cannot undo.  
**4. Tape mod last, and only as an experiment.**  
Reversible, informative, unpredictable. Do it to find out whether you like the direction, not as a permanent solution. See the box below.  
**5. Plate and mount are purchases, not mods.** Decide them before you buy the board. There is no modding your way from tray to gasket.  
**⚠ TAPE MOD — SOURCES GENUINELY DISAGREE**  
  
**The optimistic position:** it is non-permanent, peels off easily, and at worst leaves residue that isopropyl alcohol removes. This is what most enthusiast guides say.  
**The cautious position:** adhesive can degrade over time, leave residue, and in the worst case contribute to corrosion or failure on a PCB it sits directly against. Foam is non-conductive and fully reversible, so why not use foam.  
**Where this actually lands: it is a question of timeframe, not a verdict.**  
* **Short-term testing is low risk.** Days or weeks. Almost nobody disputes this.  
* **Leaving tape on a PCB for years is the genuinely untested case.** Adhesives do age, and the long-term data does not really exist.  
Worth knowing: **some of the loudest warnings come from sources that sell foam.** Discount the alarm slightly. But the underlying mechanism — adhesives age, and direct PCB contact matters — is real.  
**The practical rule: tape mod to find out whether you like the direction. If you do, get there with foam and keep that instead.**  
**The expensive mistake: over-lubing**  
  
A pack of switches runs roughly **£40–90**, and too much lubricant on tactile switches **mutes the tactility you paid for.**  
It cannot be undone without disassembling and cleaning every single switch — which is hours of work to reverse hours of work.  
**Do this instead:**  
1. Lube **one** switch.  
2. Put it in the board.  
3. **Type on it.** Not press it once — type on it.  
4. Only then do the other eighty-nine.  
**Thin coats.** You want to eliminate friction, not fill the housing. If you can see lubricant pooled anywhere, it is too much.  
**Do not lube tactile legs at all** unless you specifically want to soften the bump — which is usually the opposite of why you bought tactiles.  
**The other expensive mistake: buying the fix twice**  
  
Deciding you want a deeper sound and buying foam, tape, new keycaps and films in one order. You then have no idea which one did what, and if the result is wrong you cannot work backwards.  
**One change. Type on it for a day. Then the next.** Same principle as everything else on this chart.  
  
## PAGE 5 — "I want it to sound like ___"  
  
Reverse lookup. Find the sound you want, do the things in order, stop when you like it.  
**"Deep and thocky, not clacky"**  
  
1. Tune the stabilisers  
2. Case foam, then plate foam  
3. **PBT keycaps** — and thicker ones if you have the option  
4. Softer plate if you are buying: **POM or PC**  
5. Lube the switches  
6. Try the tape mod last  
⚠ **Honest limit:** if your board is a lightweight plastic tray-mount with an aluminium plate, you can improve it substantially but you will not get it all the way there. Some of "thock" is bought, not modded.  
**"Quieter — I share a room / I'm on calls"**  
  
1. **Silent switches.** This is the answer and nothing else comes close  
2. Case foam and plate foam  
3. **Thicker PBT keycaps**  
4. Tune the stabilisers — rattle is what carries through a wall  
5. A thick deskmat  
⚠ **Honest limit:** a normal mechanical keyboard with foam is still a mechanical keyboard. If it genuinely has to be quiet, the switch choice is the decision and everything else is a rounding error.  
**"Poppy and marbly"**  
  
1. **PE foam** — this is the mod that produces this specific sound  
2. Keep the plate on the stiffer side  
3. Lube for smoothness, not for damping  
⚠ **This sound is divisive.** Some people find the pop artificial and irritating. It is also the easiest thing on this page to reverse, so try it.  
**"Clacky and crisp — I like it loud"**  
  
1. **Aluminium or brass plate**  
2. **ABS keycaps**, thinner rather than thicker  
3. **Little or no foam** — you are removing damping, not adding it  
4. Still tune the stabilisers. Rattle is not clack, it is just noise  
**Note:** this is the one profile where doing *less* is the answer, and most guides will only tell you how to add damping.  
**"It just sounds hollow and cheap"**  
  
1. **Case foam.** This is the fix. Hollow means empty cavity  
2. If it is still hollow: PCB foam, or a tape mod to test  
3. Then stabilisers, which are probably also rattling  
Hollowness is the most fixable complaint on this page. Foam solves it almost every time.  
**"There's a ping / ring after each keypress"**  
  
1. **Spring ping** — lube the springs, or swap them  
2. **Case ring** — case foam  
3. **Plate ring** — usually brass or aluminium. Plate foam helps, a plate swap fixes it  
Identify which one it is first: press a key with the board apart if you can. The three have different fixes and guessing wastes a build.  
  
## PAGE 6 — Glossary and build checklist  
  
**Glossary**  
  
**Thock** — deep, low-pitched, muted keypress sound. The most-chased and least-defined term in the hobby.  
**Clack** — higher-pitched, brighter, sharper. Not a fault; some people want it.  
**Ping** — a metallic ringing that continues after the keypress. Usually spring, sometimes plate or case.  
**Rattle** — loose noise from a stabiliser, most obvious on the spacebar, shift and enter. Not a sound profile, just a defect.  
**Tape mod** — layers of tape applied to the *back* of the PCB.  
**Holee mod** — thin PTFE tape or fabric (**0.1–0.2 mm**) inside the stabiliser stem housing, closing the gap that ticks on the downstroke. Silk or satin glides better than cotton. Fiddly, free, reversible, and it fixes a noise nothing else will.  
**PE foam** — thin polyethylene sheet between plate and PCB. Produces the distinctive "poppy" sound.  
**Gasket mount** — plate suspended on gaskets between case halves rather than screwed to the case.  
**Tray mount** — PCB or plate screwed directly to posts in the case.  
**205g0 (Krytox)** — the standard thick lubricant for linear switch housings and stems.  
**Films** — thin shims between switch housing halves to reduce wobble.  
**WDT** — nothing to do with keyboards. If you found this here you have the espresso card.  
**Build checklist**  
  
```
BEFORE YOU START
□ Do you actually know which noise you dislike?
   (hollow / rattle / ping / scratch / too bright)
□ Have you tuned the stabilisers yet? If not, do that first
□ Have you lubed ONE switch and typed on it?

ORDER OF OPERATIONS
□ 1. Stabilisers tuned, no rattle on space/shift/enter
□ 2. Holee mod if the tick is still there
□ 3. Case foam
□ 4. Plate foam (if still too bright)
□ 5. PCB or PE foam (optional, PE is divisive)
□ 6. Switches lubed — thin, tested on one first
□ 7. Films (optional, modest effect)
□ 8. Tape mod — as an EXPERIMENT only

BEFORE YOU BUY ANYTHING
□ Plate material chosen?     ______________________
□ Mount style chosen?        ______________________
□ Keycap material chosen?    ______________________
   (these three cannot be modded, only bought)

AFTER EACH CHANGE
□ Type on it for a day before changing anything else

```
